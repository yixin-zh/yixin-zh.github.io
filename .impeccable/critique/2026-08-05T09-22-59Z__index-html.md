## Design Specificity Verdict

### LLM assessment

The portfolio is strongly authored in content and epistemic stance. Its implemented/reported/modeled/reference/needs-review grammar, concrete project artifacts, and evidence caveats are unusually specific to a research-minded robotics candidate. The paper surface, restrained evidence colors, slide rail, and academic-poster panels reinforce that intent.

Its weakness is structural: it still behaves like a generic nine-slide research-poster deck. The core promise—turn deployment failures into better robot-learning policies—does not yet govern the information architecture or primary action. A supervisor should encounter that thesis, its strongest proof, and a research-specific invitation before a broad chronology, credentials, and unrelated domains.

### Deterministic scan

The detector reported three findings in `index.html`:

1. `side-tab` at line 1263: a 3px blue left border.
2. `codex-grid-background` at line 253: a 32px two-axis linear-gradient grid.
3. `em-dash-overuse`: 85 em dashes across body text.

The grid is a contextual false positive: it fits a technical evidence-led portfolio when kept subordinate. The side-border is a real recurring motif, but the detector cannot establish that it is generic or AI-derived. The em-dash count is partly inflated by timeline and date-range metadata, yet still merits an editorial consistency pass.

### Visual overlays

No reliable user-visible overlay was available. Browser automation had no available browser connections, so no fresh tab, mutable-injection preflight, detector overlay, or console result could be produced. The fallback evidence is the successful CLI scan and source inspection.

## Overall Impression

This is a thoughtful, unusually candid portfolio with a convincing evidence ethic. Its single biggest opportunity is to turn that ethic into a research-admission narrative: make the robotics thesis, strongest proof, and contact path unmistakable before the reviewer has to work through a dense deck.

## What's Working

- The evidence taxonomy builds trust. It visibly separates what is implemented, reported, modeled, or unresolved instead of inflating claims.
- The body of work suggests a genuine path from systems engineering and deployment evidence toward robot learning rather than a list of buzzwords.
- The interaction foundation is strong: semantic slides, inactive-panel handling, skip link, keyboard navigation, URL hashes, focus styling, touch gestures, live announcements, and reduced motion are all in place.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Mobile removes the direct route to contact

At small widths, both `.mast-contact` and `.profile-links` are hidden even though they contain the only email, GitHub, and LinkedIn routes.

**Why it matters:** A mobile reviewer can become convinced but cannot immediately start the research conversation the site is meant to earn.

**Fix:** Retain a persistent plain-language action such as “Email about research / PhD opportunities” on mobile. Keep secondary links in a contact sheet or overflow.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P1] The primary research/PhD ask is too implicit

The opening identifies an R&D engineer, while the research aim sits in a secondary block and the only call to action is the generic “say hello.”

**Why it matters:** A supervisor should not need to infer whether the page is a job portfolio, general résumé, or PhD-research introduction.

**Fix:** State the research direction and desired opportunity in the first viewport, then pair it with one honest contact CTA.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Evidence is compressed or deleted instead of progressively disclosed

Poster text, labels, tags, and footnotes are specified as small as 6–8px. Small-screen rules then hide several evidence, experiment, and interpretation blocks.

**Why it matters:** Inspectable evidence is the portfolio’s differentiator. Tiny or missing evidence undermines its strongest strategic claim.

**Fix:** Give each case one readable thesis, central artifact, and evidence-status summary. Reveal methods, tables, caveats, and references through an accessible expandable detail layer or a scrollable case page; do not hide research context merely to fit a viewport.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] Mobile navigation is sequential and opaque

At tablet widths labels disappear, and on mobile the slide navigation disappears entirely, leaving only previous/next controls and an aria-hidden numeric status.

**Why it matters:** A reviewer interested in DQN or current robot systems must move through unrelated slides to find it.

**Fix:** Add a labelled Cases menu or sheet with research-first groupings, while retaining swipe and sequential controls.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] The strongest research thesis competes with breadth signals

Current robot work, climate analytics, OCR, public-code miscellany, credentials, and broad interests all occupy equal top-level status.

**Why it matters:** An admissions reviewer needs a coherent research trajectory, not proof that every past activity deserves equal attention.

**Fix:** Elevate robot systems, DQN, and research questions into a research-focus group. Move credentials and lower-confidence or adjacent work into compact supporting evidence without removing caveats.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

## Persona Red Flags

### Jordan — first-time reviewer

Within five seconds, Jordan sees a capable engineer but not an explicit PhD/research-opportunity invitation. Nine equal slide choices and a dense timeline obscure the highest-value proof path.

### Sam — accessibility-dependent reviewer

Keyboard navigation and live announcements are strong, but 6–8px research details and fixed-height, overflow-hidden slides make the evidence hard to inspect at zoom. Small-screen rules also suppress content rather than making it available on demand.

### Casey — distracted mobile reviewer

Touch target size is positive, but mobile removes direct contact and labelled case navigation. Casey can swipe through the deck but cannot jump to a research case or email the author when interrupted.

## Minor Observations

- “say hello ↗” implies an external-page affordance even though it launches `mailto:`; label it as email.
- Evidence labels use text as well as color, which is good; keep the status readable whenever its related detail is condensed.
- The final Questions slide is the right emotional close but needs a visible route to start a research conversation.
- The evidence grid is contextually appropriate; it should remain visibly subordinate.
- Rationalize the 85 em dashes, especially in prose outside date ranges.

## Questions to Consider

- If a prospective supervisor has 90 seconds, which two cases must they see before anything else?
- What would change if every screen answered one question: “Why is this candidate ready to investigate robot learning?”
- Can the evidence boundary become a confidence-building expandable layer instead of the densest visual object on the page?
