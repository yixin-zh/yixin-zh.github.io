## Publicly verifiable company snapshot

As of 2026-04-15, the public story is:

- MOYA Analytics describes itself as a Singapore climate-tech startup and an A*STAR spin-off focused on decarbonisation analytics.
- Its two public products are:
  - `GMPA` for government / policy mitigation planning
  - `Cascade` for corporate and supply-chain decarbonisation analytics
- GMPA publicly states:
  - `30 countries currently on the Atlas`
  - `150+ collaborations currently on the Atlas`
  - `10 international partners & funders`
- GMPA is described as quantifying mitigation potential at different cost levels and showing how collaboration can reduce both costs and emissions while meeting energy needs.
- MOYA's LinkedIn page currently says:
  - GMPA helps policymakers in `30+ countries`
  - MOYA's tools can identify strategies that reduce over `1 GtCO2e annually`
  - transition costs can be cut by `up to 20%`
  - Cascade helps companies account for supply-chain emissions and design cost-effective reduction pathways

Important note:

- The `30+ countries`, `>1 GtCO2e`, and `up to 20%` figures are current company claims from MOYA's LinkedIn / A*STAR report mention, not numbers I independently audited from underlying model outputs.

## Claim check: what to say more carefully

### 1. "actively used by govts like Singapore and Netherlands, and UN"

This is too strong as written.

What public sources do support:

- GMPA has partners, funders, or public speakers tied to:
  - Singapore's National Climate Change Secretariat
  - the Netherlands government / Netherlands climate officials
  - UNFCCC
  - NDC Partnership
  - ASEAN Centre for Energy
- GMPA says it works directly with policymakers and experts from countries to build and use the Atlas.

Safer phrasing:

- `used in collaboration with policymakers and partners from Singapore, the Netherlands, and UN climate institutions`
- `developed with support from Singapore and Netherlands public-sector partners and discussed with UNFCCC / NDC Partnership stakeholders`
- `supports policy analysis for countries and international partners through GMPA`

### 2. "Moya Engine"

I could not find a public reference using the exact name `Moya Engine`.

Safer phrasing:

- `the optimization / analytics backend behind GMPA`
- `the modeling and analytics backend used by GMPA`
- `MOYA's internal optimization backend`

### 3. "used by govts" vs "decision-support"

Public pages use terms like:

- `decision-support platform`
- `government collaboration mitigation planning`
- `cost-effective mitigation opportunities`
- `least-cost pathways`
- `climate planning`

Those terms fit the public record better than stronger claims about direct adoption into official policy systems.

### 4. "built the GMPA website / frontend"

This would be misleading.

What is safer to say:

- you worked on the backend / modeling / case-study side
- you were involved in beta testing an internal Unity prototype for drag-and-drop case-study creation
- you provided workflow feedback and tutorials for internal members and prospective users

Safer phrasing:

- `helped beta-test an internal Unity prototype for drag-and-drop case-study creation`
- `provided workflow feedback and usage tutorials for internal members and prospective users`
- `supported user testing of an internal case-study creation tool without claiming ownership of the public website frontend`
