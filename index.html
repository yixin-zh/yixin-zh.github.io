09 / 09 · Direction

What I want to learn next

Robot learning first: seek hard problems, shorten the learning loop, and make failures useful.

Working principle Take ownership · find the next useful step

My goal

Be challenged where robots meet research

Robot learning is my main direction. I want problems where industrial robot programs contribute fast iteration, compute, operational data, and real failure cases, while school research contributes depth, open evaluation, and room to test new ideas.

I am looking for a two-way loop in which deployment exposes better research questions and research changes the system—not application-only delivery centered mainly on business needs.

A smaller thread is decentralized multi-agent infrastructure. My practical touchpoint is the ANP handle zyx.awiki.ai for agent identity and E2EE messaging.

I am also curious about AI + formalized mathematics. My current foundation is limited to formal languages, context-free grammars, and propositional logic.

Questions I’m chasing

Make assumptions testable

  1. How can a robot verify that a physical task is actually complete?
  2. How should perception, calibration, and control error be represented and recovered?
  3. Which moments—contact, failure, intervention—are worth retaining or sharing across agents and robots?
  4. Which assumptions in LLM-driven robotics can be formalized, checked, and falsified?

What keeps me curious

Systems, ideas, and fieldwork

  • Inference-time learning, world models, action-conditioned models, and long-horizon memory.
  • Personal knowledge workflows that surface conflicts instead of silently flattening them.
  • Science translation, sustained reading, and coastal volunteering outside engineering work.

Dedicated to extreme ownership and proactive problem-solving—stepping outside my comfort zone and taking one extra step to deliver exceptional results.
